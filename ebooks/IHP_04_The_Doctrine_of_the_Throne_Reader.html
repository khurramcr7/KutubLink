In classical Arabic morphology (‘Ilm al-Ṣarf), words are not formed by random concatenation, but by projecting immutable tri-literal roots through precise mathematical matrices (Al-Mīzān aṣ-Ṣarfī).

The Isomorphic Mechanic: This mirrors formal grammar production rules and compiler generation in Computer Science. Through affine projections ($W = M \cdot R + A$), the abstract root ($R$) is transformed via morphological operators ($M$) into fully articulated lexical surface forms.

2. Physics: The Wave Mechanics of Consonantal Fields

In physics and acoustics (‘Ilm al-Aṣwāt), physical reality is defined by wave propagation, frequency spectrums, and energy distribution across space-time.

The Isomorphic Mechanic: The articulation of letters from distinct physiological exit points (Makhārij al-Ḥurūf) generates acoustic standing waves and phase interference patterns that obey Fourier harmonics.
