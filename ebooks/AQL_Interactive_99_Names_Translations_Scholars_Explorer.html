Part II — Study of the Translations: Morphological Operator Matrix & 8 Diagnostic Case Studies

Conventional English translations collapse distinct Arabic morphological weights (Awzān) into a single flat adjective. The AQL 7-Equation Lingo-Math & Al-Jurjānī Naẓm Compiler (aql_lingo_math_sovereign_compiler.py) preserves the exact operator semantics of every pattern:

1. الرَّحْمَٰن (Faʿlān) vs. الرَّحِيم (Faʿīl) — Root ر-ح-م

Faʿlān (فَعْلَان) denotes cosmic fullness and overflowing plenitude (essential, universal mercy embracing all existence, exclusive to Allāh). Faʿīl (فَعِيل) denotes permanent, abiding enactment directed toward specific recipients (33:43).

2. الْغَافِر (Fāʿil) vs. الْغَفَّار (Faʿʿāl) vs. الْغَفُور (Faʿūl) — Root غ-ف-ر

Al-Ghāfir (40:3) establishes active agency; Al-Ghaffār (5×, 20:82) signifies iterative multiplicity (forgiving sin after sin repeatedly); Al-Ghafūr (91×, 39:53) signifies qualitative depth and magnitude covering the gravest faults.

3. الْعَفُوّ (Al-ʿAfuww) vs. الْغَفُور (Al-Ghafūr) — 4:43, 4:99, 22:60

Maghfirah (غ-ف-ر) veils the fault and waives punishment; ʿAfw (ع-ف-و) completely obliterates and erases the trace of the fault as wind erases footprints in sand. Hence عَفُوًّا غَفُورًا ascends in reassurance.

4. الْخَالِقُ الْبَارِئُ الْمُصَوِّرُ (59:24) — 3-Stage Creative Architecture

Per Al-Ghazālī's architectural analogy: Al-Khāliq determines and proportions the blueprint (Taqdīr); Al-Bāriʾ brings it into existence free of flaw (Ījād); Al-Muṣawwir (Form II) bestows distinct individual form and identity.

5. الْقَادِر (Fāʿil) vs. الْقَدِير (Faʿīl) vs. الْمُقْتَدِر (Muftaʿil, Form VIII)

Per Ziyādat al-Mabnā Tadullu ʿalā Ziyādat al-Maʿnā, Form VIII Al-Muqtadir (54:42, 54:55) expresses consummate, enacted omnipotence beyond active capability (Qādir) and permanent power (Qadīr).

6. الْقَاهِر (Fāʿil, 6:18) vs. الْقَهَّار (Faʿʿāl, 6×)

Every single occurrence of ٱلْقَهَّارُ in the Qur'ān (6/6) is immediately preceded by ٱلْوَٰحِدُ (12:39, 13:16, 14:48, 38:65, 39:4, 40:16), proving that true Oneness necessitates irresistible dominion over all things.

7. الْوَاحِد (Al-Wāḥid, 30×) vs. الْأَحَد (Al-Aḥad, 112:1)

Al-Wāḥid affirms uniqueness without partner in Lordship and Worship (2:163); Al-Aḥad (112:1) affirms indivisible singularity of Essence that admits no composition, partition, or equal.

8. Al-Jurjānī's Naẓm Law of Verse-Final Fawāṣil (5:38 vs. 24:5)

In Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz, Imām al-Jurjānī proves that verse-final Divine Names are governed by syntactic causality: judicial recompense (5:38) must close with عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ (Might + Wisdom), whereas repentance (5:39) closes with غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ.
